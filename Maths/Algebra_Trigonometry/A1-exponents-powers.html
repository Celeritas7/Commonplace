Exponents & Powers

A power is repeated multiplication written short. Zero and negative powers follow from one rule: each step down the ladder divides by the base.

§1

What a power is

25 means multiply 2 by itself five times:

25=2×2×2×2×2=32

The base is the number being multiplied (2). The exponent says how many times (5). Read it as “2 to the power 5”.

Going down the ladder

Each time the exponent drops by one, the value is divided by the base:

10310210110010−110−2​=1000=100=10=1=101​=1001​​
Two rules from the ladder
a0=1  for any a=0
a−m=am1​  — a negative power means “one over”

So a−m and am are multiplicative inverses: their product is 1.

Common traps
• 2−3 is not negative: it equals 81​.
• (−2)4=16 but −24=−16 — the bracket decides what is raised.
• am+an=am+n. The laws work for multiplying, not adding.
• An odd power of a negative stays negative: (−3)3=−27.

The laws

These hold for any integer exponents, positive, zero or negative.

Same base → add or subtract the exponents. Same exponent → combine the bases.

§3

Standard form

Very large or very small numbers are written as

k×10n

where 1≤k<10 and n is an integer.

  • Mass of the Earth: 5.97×1024 kg
  • Thickness of a sheet of paper: 0.000016 m =1.6×10−5 m

How to convert: move the decimal point until one non-zero digit sits to its left, and count the moves. Moved left (a big number) → positive n. Moved right (a small number) → negative n.

Worked examples

Try each one in your notebook first, then open the steps.

Example 1 — Simplify (−3)−2×(−3)5÷(−3)2
Same base, so work with the exponents only: −2+5−2=1.
Answer: (−3)1=−3.
Example 2 — Find m if 3m×3−4=32
Left side is 3m−4. Equal bases, so equal exponents: m−4=2.
Answer: m=6.
Example 3 — Evaluate (21​)−3+4−1
(21​)−3=23=8 (flip, make the power positive). 4−1=41​.
Answer: 841​=433​.
Example 4 — (6×104)×(5×10−7) in standard form
Numbers: 6×5=30. Powers: 4+(−7)=−3. So 30×10−3.
But 30 is not between 1 and 10: 30=3×101, giving 3×10−2.

§6

Where this shows up in ML

  • Learning rates and tolerances are tiny powers of ten: lr=1e-3 is 10−3.
  • Python's e notation is standard form: 5.97e24 =5.97×1024.
  • Exponential decay: learning-rate schedules shrink by a constant factor each step (Problem 19).
  • ex sits inside the sigmoid and softmax functions. Its laws are exactly these laws, with base e≈2.718.
Looking ahead · logarithms
A logarithm undoes a power: since 103=1000, log10​1000=3. You'll meet it in log-loss, the cost function of logistic regression.
