Section 6 · Summarize and report · F5A

Turn a register into a useful summary

Add amounts for selected records, apply two conditions together, and count matching records without adding their values.

One condition, two conditions, or a count?

SUMIF adds numbers for matching records: =SUMIF(criteria_range,criterion,sum_range).

SUMIFS adds numbers when every condition matches: =SUMIFS(sum_range,range1,criterion1,range2,criterion2). The sum range comes first, unlike SUMIF.

COUNTIF counts matching cells: =COUNTIF(range,criterion). A matching record with an amount of zero still counts as one record.

Use equally sized ranges so records line up. A formula that returns zero might mean there are no matches, or the matching amounts add to zero. Check the matched cells as well as the result.

Criteria and result cells

Practice subset: contiguous text ranges in B2:C7 and numeric sum ranges in D2:D7; every supplied range must have the same length. Use F2/F3 or quoted exact text (1–32 supported ASCII letters, digits, spaces, hyphens or underscores, starting with a letter). Text matching ignores letter case. Sums require valid numbers in the selected sum range; COUNTIF ignores amounts. No wildcard or comparison criteria, nested formulas, or extra SUMIFS pairs. Native Excel supports more options.
