§03 Bending

Bend allowance and K-factor

Source

Bend a flat strip and its outside stretches while its inside is squeezed. Somewhere between the two is a layer that keeps its original length. That layer, the neutral axis, decides how long the blank must be.

In a gentle bend the neutral axis sits near mid-thickness. In a tight bend the sheet thins a little and the neutral axis moves toward the inside of the bend. The K-factor records where it ends up: K = δ / t, where δ is its distance from the inside surface. K = 0.5 is the middle; real bends sit between about 0.3 and 0.5.

The four lengths

mm · θ in radians
BA = θ · (R + K·t)·OSSB = tan(θ/2) · (R + t)·BD = 2·OSSB − BA·Flat = A + B − BD

Drawings usually dimension flanges to the virtual sharp, the point where the two outside surfaces would meet if the corner were sharp. Measured that way, the corner is counted twice. The real metal takes the shorter path round the arc, so the blank is shorter than A + B by the bend deduction.

Three things to hold

Definition
K = δ / t

δ is the distance from the inside surface to the neutral axis. K stays at or below 0.5.

Angle
θ is the bend

Use the angle the sheet turns through, not the angle between the flanges. A 120° part angle is a 60° bend.

Creo
Y = K · π/2

Creo asks for a Y-factor. Its default, Y = 0.5, means K ≈ 0.32. Set it from your shop's test bends.

In practice

✓ CORRECT

Thickness, radius and Y-factor set in the Creo part from test bends on the real press brake. The flat pattern matches the formed part, and the drawing states the K used.

✗ INCORRECT

Cutting the blank at A + B. For 2 mm steel, R 2, a 90° bend, that blank is 3.6 mm too long, and every flange after the first bend comes out long.

The bend angle is how far the sheet turns, so θ = 180° − 120° = 60°.

Q2Creo is left at its default Y-factor of 0.5. What K-factor is that?
Show working

Y = K·π/2, so K = 2Y/π = 1/π ≈ 0.32. That suits tight bends; larger radii usually need a higher value.
