Control-Wiring Conventions

SOURCE — MACHINE-TOOL ELECTRICAL DESIGN HANDBOOK PP. 737–739 · TABLES 324–326

Control-cabinet wiring has its own drawing conventions, older than the automotive ones and stricter: every conductor is numbered to the circuit diagram at every termination, and colour names the circuit class, not the function.

Numbering
Conductor ↔ diagram
Every conductor is marked at each termination with the number on the circuit diagram; terminal blocks carry permanent matching marks. Series/parallel switch chains return to terminals forming intermediate test points, shown on the diagram.
Earthing
Main terminal sizing
Main earthing terminal beside the input terminals; sized equal to the supply conductor up to 16 mm², at least 50% of it (min 16 mm²) above. Every internal earth wire lands on that one terminal. Cable grade: over 250 V to earth → 650/1100 V insulation.

⚠ THREE COLOUR SYSTEMS, ONE SHOP
Note the collisions: automotive DC (§05 sheet 01) uses white for ground; control cabinets use green; AC uses white for neutral. Red is a hot on AC, a control class here, and a warning lamp everywhere. The drawing must say which convention governs — never assume from the wire.
