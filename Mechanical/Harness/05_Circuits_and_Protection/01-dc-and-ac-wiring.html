§05 Circuits & Protection · Sheet CP-01

DC & AC Wiring Practice

SOURCE — CALTRANS ELECTRIC STANDARD §1.2–1.3 (IPC-A-620 CLASS 2)

Vehicle circuits split into two worlds with different failure stakes: 12/24 V DC, where the enemy is voltage drop and corrosion, and 120/240 V AC, where the enemy is people. The rules diverge accordingly.

DC · 12/24 V
Ground-wire return preferred
Chassis return only below the OEM frame-current limit (~30 A); above it, run a dedicated negative wire. Ground lugs on bare metal, then sealed with corrosion-resistant film — never a toothed washer through paint. Hinged doors get a flexible ground strap; hinges don’t count.
AC · 120/240 V
Everything in metal conduit
Stranded THHN or UL-listed wire only, run in liquid-tight flexible steel or EMT conduit, supported every 18”. Every receptacle behind a GFCI, grounding type, never face-up. One ground connection per system; conduit is never the ground path.

✓ DC MINIMUMS
Stranded copper only · 18 ga floor for electrical, 22 ga for electronics · SXL/GXL/TXL under hood, GPT/HDT in the cab · fine-strand marine cable where it flexes · drip loops on exposed runs · bend radius ≥ 5× diameter.
✗ NEVER
Wire nuts or scotchlocks anywhere · solid-core wire on a vehicle · splices in flex zones · tapping engine/transmission control wires without written approval · adhesive-backed tie holders · AC and DC sharing plugs or receptacles.
