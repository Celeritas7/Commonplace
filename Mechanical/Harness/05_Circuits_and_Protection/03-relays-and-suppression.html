§05 Circuits & Protection · Sheet CP-03

Relays, Solenoids & Switches

SOURCE — CALTRANS ELECTRIC STANDARD §6, §8

Every coil in the harness is a stored-energy device. Cut its current and the collapsing field drives a back-EMF spike hundreds of volts high — straight into whatever switched it. Suppression is not optional; the standard requires it at the coil.

FIG. CP-03 — SUPPRESSION AT THE COIL
RELAY
COIL + DIODE, SAME HOUSING
Suppressor options — diode · resistor · varistor · zener-zener
Location — at the coil, built-in or exterior-mounted
DC motors — blocking diode; if relay-driven, diode on the relay coil
110%
Pull-in margin
Supply at least 110% of the minimum rated energising voltage — a relay fed through a long thin wire may chatter instead of closing.
90%
Drop-out margin
To release, the coil must fall below 90% of maximum rated dropout voltage — leakage paths that hold a coil half-alive are a design fault.
Derate
By load type
Contact ratings assume a load type. Switching mixed loads? Derate for the dominant one. Mechanical relays in vibration zones need special mounting — no unintended switching, ever.

✓ SWITCH RULES
Voltage rating ≥ system voltage · rated for the current and the load type · screw terminals #6 minimum · branch-circuit switches live in the positive wire · IP65 switch + enclosure anywhere rain or spray reaches.
✗ THE UNSUPPRESSED COIL
One bare solenoid coil sharing a harness with an ECU is how “random” electronics faults are born — the spike conducts and induces into every neighbouring wire. If you can’t find the suppressor, assume there isn’t one.
