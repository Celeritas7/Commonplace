Clamping & Routing

SOURCE — WIRING FUNDAMENTALS COURSE (AC 43.13-1B PRACTICE)

A harness is only as reliable as its supports. Clamps carry the bundle's weight, keep it off structure and moving parts, and stop vibration from working the conductors. Two families do most of the work: plastic snap-in tie mounts and cushioned rubber clamps.

Plastic snap-in clamp (tie mount)

Four working parts: the release tab, the support bracket, the tail, and the snap-in tie mount itself. The tie wraps the bundle; the mount snaps into the panel hole and can be released by the tab for rework.

Rubber-cushioned clamp

The bundle sits fully inside the rubber cushion — all wires contained, no pinching at the clamp tabs. Use a stand-off to hold the run clear of structure, and a wedge to fill dead space so the bundle cannot shuffle.

RULES
90±5°
Clamp orientation to the run — the clamp sits square to the bundle, within ±5°. Anything else distorts the cushion and chafes insulation.
TAB → SLOT
Engage the clamp tab in its slot before torquing. A tab riding outside the slot lets the clamp rotate under vibration.
NO PINCH
No conductor trapped between clamp tabs or between cushion and bracket. Every wire inside the cushion, none under the fastener.
✓ CORRECT
Clamp square to the run, tab engaged, cushion round, bundle snug — clamp takes the load, wires take none.
✗ INCORRECT
Clamp distorted by over-tightening or skewed mounting — cushion collapses, wires pinch at the tabs and chafe through.
