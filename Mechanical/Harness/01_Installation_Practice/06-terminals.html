Terminals & Joint Strength

SOURCE — WIRING FUNDAMENTALS COURSE (AC 43.13-1B PRACTICE)

A terminal exists to make the end of a wire behave like the middle of it. Two acceptance criteria say everything: the joint must be at least as strong as the wire, and its resistance must vanish next to the wire's own.

Fjoint ≥ Fwire
Mechanical criterion
Tensile strength of the wire-to-terminal joint should be at least the equivalent tensile strength of the wire itself. Pull to failure — the wire should break, not the crimp slip.
Rjoint ≈ 0
Electrical criterion
Resistance of the joint should be negligible relative to the normal resistance of the wire. A resistive crimp is a heater — see the overheated-splice defect on Sheet 05.
