§02 Wires & Cables · Sheet JW-02

Conductors & Stranding

SOURCE — JUDD WIRE AUTOMOTIVE CATALOG · CONDUCTOR REFERENCE PP. 1–4

Two choices define a conductor: what the copper is (material and coating) and how it is twisted (stranding pattern). The first sets conductivity, corrosion and solderability; the second sets flex life, diameter control and crimp quality.

BC
Bare copper · 100% IACS
Cheapest, best conductivity — but oxidizes rapidly and is hard to solder once dull. Crimps well.
TC
Tinned · 93–97% IACS
Tin plating resists oxidation and solders easily. Solderability fades with shelf life and heat aging.
CAL
Copper alloy · 83% IACS
490 MPa tensile, ~4.5× the flex endurance of annealed copper — the ABS-sensor-cable conductor. Crimp, don’t solder.
SPC
Silver plated · 200 °C
Full conductivity, low RF loss, stays solderable after heat aging. Very expensive — reserved for hot zones.

⚠ COATING VARIANTS
Top coat — bare strands twisted, tinned overall. Heavy tinned — strands doubly tinned, then twisted. Overcoat — individually tinned strands, twisted, tinned again overall. Fused — doubly tinned, twisted, then fused solid. Silicone-blocked variants (SBB/SBT) press sealant between strands to stop fluid wicking — standard inside transmissions and fuel zones.
