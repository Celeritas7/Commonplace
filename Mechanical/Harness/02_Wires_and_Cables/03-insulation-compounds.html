Insulation Compounds

SOURCE — JUDD WIRE AUTOMOTIVE CATALOG · INSULATION REFERENCE P. 5

Every insulation compound belongs to one of three families, and the family tells you how it dies: thermoplastics melt, thermosets char but hold shape, elastomers stretch and recover. Cross-linking (the “XL” prefix) is how a cheap thermoplastic gets promoted into a thermoset.

Thermoplastic
TP · PVC · HDPE · PA
Not cross-linked. Melts repeatedly on heating, dissolves in solvents. Low cost, reprocessable.
Thermoset
TS · XLPE · XLPVC · XLFE
Fully cross-linked — will not melt again. Better heat, tensile, cut-through, abrasion and chemical resistance.
Elastomer
E · TPE · PU
Chemically or physically cross-linked, properties in between. High elasticity and elongation — jackets that flex.
FIG. JW-03 — WHAT THE ELECTRON BEAM DOES
Before — free polymer chains · melts · dissolves
After — cross-linked network · thermoset behavior

High-velocity electrons break chemical bonds in the insulation, liberating free radicals that re-form as links between neighbouring chains. The result resists cut-through, deformation and chemical attack — and won’t shrink back or melt against a 349 °C soldering iron, so solder rework can’t damage it.

✓ RULE OF THUMB
XLPE is the workhorse — body and chassis wiring to 150 °C, fuel-resistant grades available. Step up to XLFE only where fluid immersion and 200 °C demand it.
✗ WATCH FOR
Plain PVC and TPE near heat sources — both are back to liquid around their rating. And halogenated compounds (PVC family) where halogen-free is specified.
