§02 Wires & Cables · Sheet JW-06

Resistance & Voltage Drop

SOURCE — JUDD WIRE AUTOMOTIVE CATALOG · CONDUCTOR METRIC REFERENCE P. 3

The last column of every conductor table — Ω/km at 20 °C — is the one that actually sizes a circuit. On a 12 V system there are only a few volts to spend; a wire chosen one size too thin gives them away as heat before the load ever sees them.

V = I · R · L
The whole calculation
Current (A) × table resistance (Ω/km) × circuit length (km). Count the full loop — feed and return.
20 °C
Table condition
Copper resistance rises ~0.4%/°C — an engine-bay run at 100 °C carries ~30% more resistance than the table value.
−½ size
Coated & alloy penalty
TC runs 93–97% IACS, copper alloy 83% — the starred table rows carry measurably more Ω/km for the same mm².

12 V M GND
✗ 0.5 mm² · 36.7 Ω/km
R = 36.7 × 0.005 km = 0.184 Ω
V = 10 A × 0.184 = 1.84 V lost
Load sees 10.2 V — 15% gone
✓ 2.0 mm² · 9.30 Ω/km
R = 9.30 × 0.005 km = 0.047 Ω
V = 10 A × 0.047 = 0.47 V lost
Load sees 11.5 V — healthy
Loop length = 2.5 m out + 2.5 m back = 5 m = 0.005 km

✗ CLASSIC MISTAKE
Measuring only the feed wire. The ground path — return wire, splices, chassis strap — is in series with the load too. A corroded ground adds resistance the calculation never saw.
