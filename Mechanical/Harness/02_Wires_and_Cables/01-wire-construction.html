§02 Wires & Cables · Sheet JW-01

Wire Construction

SOURCE — JUDD WIRE AUTOMOTIVE CATALOG · CONSTRUCTION & PROCESS SHEETS

A harness wire is not one thing but a stack of layers, each solving a different failure mode. The copper carries current; everything wrapped around it exists to survive heat, fluid, abrasion and pinch. Read any wire spec from the inside out.

Conductor
Layer 1 · the work
Stranded copper — bare, tin-coated or alloy. Strand count and pattern set flex life and crimp quality.
Insulation
Layer 2 · dielectric
The primary wall over each conductor. Compound choice sets the temperature rating — PVC to fluoroelastomer.
Filler
Layer 3 · optional
Fills the voids between twisted conductors so the cable stays round and the jacket doesn’t collapse into the gaps.
Jacket
Layer 4 · armor
The outer sheath. Takes the abrasion, pinch, cut-through and chemical attack so the insulation never has to.

Specialty cables (sensor, databus, battery) build the full stack: conductors → insulation → filler → drain wire → binder tape → tape separator → braided shield → outer jacket. The drain wire rides in contact with the shield so the shield can be terminated to ground without combing out the braid.

PRODUCTION PROCESS — COPPER ROD TO SHIPPED REEL
Copper rod→ Drawing→ Tinning→ Bunching→ Extruding→ Irradiation→ Twisting · jacketing · shielding→ Rewinding · shipping
⚠ WHY IRRADIATION MATTERS
Electron-beam cross-linking turns the extruded insulation from a thermoplastic into a thermoset — it will no longer melt or shrink back, even against a soldering iron at 349 °C. Cut-through, deformation and chemical resistance all improve. Simple wires ship straight after irradiation; cables go on to twisting, shielding and jacketing first.
