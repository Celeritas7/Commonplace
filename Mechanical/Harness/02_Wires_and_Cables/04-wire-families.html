§02 Wires & Cables · Sheet JW-04

Wire Families

SOURCE — JUDD WIRE AUTOMOTIVE CATALOG · ABS / TRANSMISSION / PRIMARY / SPECIALTY SHEETS

The catalog divides automotive wire into four families by where it lives on the vehicle. Each family is really a temperature-and-abuse profile: how hot, how much flexing, what fluid it soaks in.

ABS cable
150 °C cont · 200 °C molding
Jacketed twisted pairs for wheel-speed and ride-control sensors. Copper-alloy conductors for extreme flex life at the moving corner of the car.
Transmission
200 °C cont · 250 °C short
Lives immersed in 150 °C fluid. XLFE/ETFE insulation, silicone-blocked conductors (SBT/SBB) so oil can’t wick up the strands.
Primary
−55 to 150 °C
The chassis-harness workhorse. SAE grades TXL/GXL/SXL and JASO grades AVSSX/AVX/AEX — same copper, different wall.
Specialty
−55 to 250 °C
Everything with a shield, drain or special duty — airbag ignitor, gas-tank, fuselink, databus, battery cable.

⚠ READING THE GRADE LETTERS
SAE J1128: first letter = wall (Thin, General, Special/heavy), “X” = cross-linked, “L” = low-tension. JASO mirrors it in metric: AVSSX (super-thin XLPVC), AVX (standard XLPVC), AEX (XLPE, 120 °C). A trailing “f” on a JASO size means a flexible-strand conductor.
