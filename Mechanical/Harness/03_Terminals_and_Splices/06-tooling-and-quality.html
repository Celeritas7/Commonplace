Tooling & Quality

SOURCE — AMP CATALOG 82042 · PERFORMANCE SPECS PP. 4–5 · TOOLING PP. 66–70

Two numbers prove a termination: tensile strength (does it hold?) and millivolt drop (does it conduct?). MIL-T-7928 tests both after the abuse — current cycling, vibration, salt corrosion — and a healthy crimp barely moves.

Ratchet
CERTI-CRIMP hand tools
Won’t release until the dies fully bottom — a half-squeezed crimp physically cannot leave the tool.
Locator
In the tool head
Positions the part against its notch so the crimp lands on the barrel — no twisting, no off-centre strikes.
Die marks
Built-in QC
TERMINYL dies stamp the wire size into the crimped barrel — the finished joint records which die made it.

✓ WHAT THE NUMBERS MEAN
A good crimp’s millivolt drop after vibration and corrosion is half the allowable spec — the joint isn’t degrading, it’s stable. Rising drop over service life is the first sign of a loose or contaminated crimp.
✗ TOOLING IS NOT OPTIONAL
Every family calls out its tooling: PIDG tools for PIDG, TETRA-CRIMP for FASTON, dedicated hand tools per CERTI-SEAL size. The published tensile and millivolt numbers exist only for the matched pair.
