Minimum bend radius

How tight a hose may bend, and why the outside of the bend decides.

Reading time8 min
Includes2 calculators · 3 questions

Every hose catalogue lists a (MBR) for each size. It is the tightest radius the hose can be bent to and still reach its rated pressure and service life. Bend it tighter and nothing breaks on the day. The hose just fails sooner, usually at the outside of the bend.

Catalogue checkMakers do not all measure MBR the same way. Some measure to the hose centreline, some to the inside of the bend. Read the definition line before you compare it with a radius from Creo.

The reason is the . A hose carries pressure in its braided or spiral wire layers. On the outside of a bend those layers are stretched apart and open up gaps. On the inside they are pushed together. The rubber tube ovalises, the bore narrows, and every pressure pulse works the stretched side hardest.

01Reading the number

For a hose of outside radius r bent to centreline radius R, the outer wall stretches by about r / R. A Ø12 hose at R 65 stretches its outer wall by roughly 9 %. At R 40 it is 15 %. The catalogue's MBR is simply the point where the maker stops guaranteeing the hose.

Flexing serviceIf one end of the hose moves, the bend works every cycle. Catalogues often give a larger dynamic value, or ask for extra margin. Use that number, not the static one. Try the Flex button in Fig. 1 at R 70.

Treat the MBR as a floor, never as a target. In Creo, set the single radius a little above it, for example 66 for a 65 limit, so rounding in the model never leaves a bend just under the limit.

02Two bends on one straight

A rounded corner uses up some straight hose on each side of it. That length is the , and it grows quickly as the corner gets sharper. Two neighbouring corners must fit both tangent lengths inside the straight between them, with room left for a clip.

03In practice

✓ HOLDS THE RADIUSCorner points at every turn, Creo's single radius set just above MBR, clips placed on straights. Every bend measures exactly R.
✗ LOOKS SMOOTH, ISN'TA spline through a few stations. Between close stations it tightens, and the check finds bends at R 59.8, 60.4 and 61.3 against a 65 limit.
Seen at workAll three tight bends in one real route came from a spline. Re-routing those sections with corner points at R 66 cleared them.
