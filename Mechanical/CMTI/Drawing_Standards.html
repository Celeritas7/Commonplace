Drawing sheet sizes p.83

The A-series (IS 696-1972) is built on two rules: halving (each size is half the area of the one above) and format similarity — the sides keep the ratio Y = X·√2, so a sheet halved stays the same shape. A0 is exactly 1 m². The filing margin is 20 mm on the left of A4; other borders are 5 mm. A standard title block sits bottom-right (≈185 × 67 mm).

Orthographic projections p.84

Two systems are permitted. In first-angle projection the object sits between the observer and the plane, so each view is pushed to the far side of the front view (top view below, left view to the right). In third-angle projection the plane is between observer and object, so views fall on the near side (top view above, left view to the left). The truncated-cone symbol in the title block tells the reader which system is used.

Conventional representation p.85–86

IS 696-1972 fixes simplified conventions so common features need not be drawn in full. Each is shown as the actual projection alongside the agreed convention. Features covered:

  • Screw threads (external & internal)
  • Small intersections of cylinders
  • Coil springs (compression, several forms)
  • Repeated holes on a circular or linear pitch
  • Flats and square ends on shafts
  • Radial ribs / spokes (one omitted or revolved into plane)
  • Sections through ribs (not hatched)
  • End views of splines and serrations
  • Spiral springs and disc springs
  • Straight and diamond knurling
  • Bearings

Graphical symbols for kinematic schemes p.95–100

A kinematic scheme is a skeleton diagram of a machine's drive — shafts, bearings, gears, clutches and controls drawn as agreed symbols rather than to scale. The standard set is grouped as below.

Geometrical tolerances — symbols & interpretation p.101–102

The tolerance frame on a drawing names a characteristic, a tolerance value, and (for related features) a datum. Each characteristic defines a tolerance zone the feature must lie within. The full set:

Geometric tolerances — single (form) features
CharacteristicSymbolTolerance zone
Straightness—Axis or line lies between two parallel straight lines (or within a cylinder) apart by the tolerance.
Flatness▱Surface lies between two parallel planes apart by the tolerance.
Circularity (roundness)○Each cross-section lies between two concentric circles whose radial difference = tolerance.
Cylindricity⌭Surface lies between two coaxial cylinders whose radial difference = tolerance.
Profile of a line⌒Profile lies between two lines enveloping circles of dia = tolerance, centred on the true profile.
Profile of a surface⌓Surface lies between two surfaces enveloping spheres of dia = tolerance, centred on the true surface.
