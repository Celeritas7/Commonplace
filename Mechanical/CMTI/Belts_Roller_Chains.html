Belts & Roller Chains

Machine Elements · printed pp. 227–252
Notebook 15. Flat-belt fundamentals, the V-belt section series with selection basics, and roller-chain geometry & selection. Power ratings per belt/chain are catalogue data — the dense rating grids are OCR slots; formulas and dimensions here are standard.

Flat belts p.227–232

T₁ / T₂ = eµθ    P = (T₁ − T₂)·v T₁, T₂ = tight / slack tensions, µ ≈ 0.3 leather on CI, θ = arc of contact (rad), v = belt speed. Best economy at v ≈ 15–20 m/s.
  • Arc of contact on the small pulley: θ = 180° − 60°·(D − d)/a; keep ≥ 120°.
  • Pulleys are crowned (≈ 0.3–1 mm per 100 mm width) — the belt self-centres on the high point.
  • Centre distance a ≈ 1.5–2 × (D + d); an idler on the slack side raises θ where a is short.

V-belts p.233–243

The 40° wedge multiplies friction (effective µ′ = µ/sin 20° ≈ 3µ), so V-belts carry more power at shorter centres. Sections per IS 2494:

Groove angle 34°–38°, increasing with pulley dia so the belt seats without bottoming. Never mix new and run-in belts in one set.
Pitch length:  L = 2a + (π/2)·(D + d) + (D − d)² / 4a Round to the nearest standard nominal length; recompute a from the chosen L. Apply a service factor 1.1–1.4 (drive + duty) to the design power.

Length in pitches:  Lp = 2a/p + (z₁ + z₂)/2 + [ (z₂ − z₁) / 2π ]²·p/a Round Lp up to an even number (avoids the cranked offset link). Sprocket pitch dia: d = p / sin(180°/z).
  • Small sprocket z₁ ≥ 17–19 (chordal action falls with z); ratio ≤ 7 per stage.
  • Centre distance a ≈ 30–50 pitches; keep the slack strand on the bottom.
  • Lubrication by speed: drip < 4 m/s · bath 4–7 m/s · forced feed above.
