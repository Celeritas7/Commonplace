Toothed (positive) clutches p.186–209

Jaw and gear-tooth clutches transmit by form, not friction — no slip, small size for the torque, but they engage only near synchronism:

  • Square jaws — drive both directions; engage at rest or < 10 rev/min relative.
  • Sawtooth / spiral jaws — one direction, self-engaging; ratchet at low speed.
  • Gear-tooth (dog) clutches — external/internal tooth rings, as in feed gearboxes; engage below ≈ 0.7 m/s relative tooth speed.

Check tooth flanks for bearing pressure and the shifting fork groove for wear; chamfer tooth ends for easier engagement.

Friction clutches p.210–218

T = µ · W · Rm · n W = axial force, n = number of friction surfaces (plates − 1 pairs), Rm = (Ro + Ri)/2 (uniform-wear basis). Size for ≈ 1.3–1.5 × the running torque.

Multi-plate clutches run wet in machine-tool headstocks: lower µ but even wear and easy heat removal. Keep Ri/Ro ≈ 0.6–0.7.

Brakes p.219–226

Band brake:  T = (T₁ − T₂)·r,   T₁/T₂ = eµθ Differential band arrangements can self-energise — check they cannot self-lock. Shoe brakes: T = µ·N·r per shoe, with N from the lever geometry.
Thermal check

Limit the pressure–velocity product: p·v ≲ 10–30 (kgf/cm²·m/s) for lined shoes — lower for continuous duty, higher for occasional stops. Energy per stop = ½·I·ω² must dissipate through the drum surface without exceeding ≈ 250 °C at the lining.
