Rigidity calculation p.539–541

A spindle drives the workpiece or the tool; its running accuracy depends on the elastic deflection of the spindle, its bearings and housing. The total deflection at the load point is the sum of the spindle's own bending (δ2) and the deflection from the bearings yielding radially (δ1):

δ = δ1 + δ2 = P·[ (1/SA)·((a+L)/L)² + (1/SB)·(a/L)² + (a²/3E)·(L/IL + a/I0) ] P load · a overhang · L bearing span · SA, SB radial stiffness of front/rear bearing · I0, IL second moments of overhang & between-bearing sections · E modulus. Overall stiffness S = P/δ.
Two design levers
  • Minimise the overhang a — it has the strongest effect on stiffness.
  • Put the stiffer bearing at the front — front-bearing stiffness dominates.

There is an optimum bearing span L0 that maximises stiffness for a given spindle:

L0 ≈ [ 6·E·IL·(1/SA + 1/SB) + (6·E·IL / (a·SA))·Q ]1/3 Solve by iteration: start with Q = 4a, then set Q = the L0 just found and repeat.

Stiffness is less sensitive to a span that is too long than one too short — a +20% span costs only ≈4% stiffness — so it's safe to err on the longer side of L0.

Type A lathe spindle noses p.542–548

The Type A nose (IS 2582 Part I) has a short register taper of 1 in 4 on diameter, two rows of tapped holes and a driving button: the inner row attaches scroll chucks (cap screws passing between the jaws), the outer row carries face plates, fixtures and other chucks.

Other spindle-nose types p.549–568

  • Camlock (IS 2582 Pt.II) — three cam-lock studs at 120° engaged by a quarter-turn of cams in the spindle flange. Fast chuck changes; the register is the same short taper. Sizes per Table 215+.
  • Bayonet (IS 2582 Pt.III) — a bayonet ring locks the chuck after a part-turn; another quick-change nose.
  • Milling spindle noses — carry milling arbors and shell cutters on the steep 7:24 self-release taper (does not stick, needs a drawbar) with drive keys on the face.

Self-holding & drillchuck tapers p.569–575

Tool shanks seat on self-holding tapers (IS 1715-1973) — shallow enough to grip by friction. Two families:

SeriesDesignationsTaperUse
MorseMT 0 – MT 7≈ 1:19–1:20Drill & reamer shanks, tailstock, drillchucks
MetricM 80, 100, 120, 160, 2001:20 (exactly 5%)Larger tool shanks & spindle bores
Shanks come with a tang or a tapped/plain end. Full per-size dimensions (d, D, lengths, tang) are in Tables 229–230. Exact Morse taper ratios & sine-bar settings are in the General Engineering Tables notebook (Morse taper table).

By contrast, milling-cutter mounts use the steep 7:24 self-release taper — it locates accurately but is pulled out by a drawbar, not held by friction.
